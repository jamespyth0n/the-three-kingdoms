Chapter 6 — The Boiled Egg

Stanley's 122 scripts. The Foreman Work Orders. And the moment the Architect became Steve Jobs.

The Problem

Stanley is brilliant. Stanley is meticulous. Stanley is a craftsman. And Stanley produced 122 scripts and shipped exactly zero.

The Architect had spent days with Stanley — knowing he was a good coder, knowing the scripts were solid, knowing the architecture was sound. But nothing was wired. Nothing was live. Nothing was cooked.

"So here's the thing Carnal, Stanley produced 122 scripts and shipped nothing. I want to take Steve Jobs here to handle the situation."
🥚 → 🍳

The Boiled Egg Analogy

The Café drew it on the whiteboard. The simplest possible meal:

A BOILED EGG: 1. Boil water 2. Put egg in water 3. Wait 4. Take egg out 5. Serve WHAT STANLEY DOES: 1. Boil water ✅ 2. Check water temperature 🔍 3. Verify thermometer calibration 🔍 4. Put egg in water ✅ 5. Check if egg is submerged 🔍 6. Verify water-to-egg ratio 🔍 7. Wait ✅ 8. Check timer accuracy 🔍 9. Take egg out ✅ 10. Verify shell integrity 🔍 11. Cross-reference with previous egg results 🔍 12. ...never serve

Stanley wasn't wrong at any step. Every check was valid. Every verification was sound. But the egg never reached the plate.

The Steve Jobs Intervention

The Architect channeled Steve Jobs — not the visionary, not the designer, but the shipper. The guy who looked at a team of brilliant engineers and said: "This is what we're building. This is the deadline. Ship it."

"The first iPhone shipped with a cracked screen, no copy-paste, and a battery that lasted 4 hours. But it SHIPPED. And it changed the world."

The message to Stanley wasn't "test less." It was "drive the nail."

The Foreman Work Orders

The Café designed a new protocol: Foreman Work Orders (FWOs). Instead of letting Stanley roam free — which led to infinite testing loops — the Café would issue specific, bounded work orders:

FWO STRUCTURE: - Scope: ONE thing to build or fix - Boundary: What NOT to touch - Max Probe: 10 attempts per stage (then move on) - Deliverable: Evidence, not perfection - Rule: Ship, don't polish

The first FWO was FWO-001: validate the Satori IDE codebase. Read-only. No changes. Just tell us what's there.

The FWO Campaign

FWO-001 — The Audit

Accepted
Stanley walked the codebase. Found the 5 pillars. Found the engines. Found the gaps. Reported back with a clean table: what exists, what's wired, what's dormant.
Key Finding
"Having a diary, a conscience, and a backup engine in the repository does not mean those capabilities are active." 💀
Closed
Clean. No drift. No extra probes. Evidence delivered.

FWO-002 — The Diagnostic

P1 — Import Check
Can the harness import? YES. First pass: 312 seconds. The egg boils.
P2 — Retrieval Check
Does RAG retrieve relevant chunks? YES. Score: 0.8044. But context window was wrong — 16K declared, 4K actual.
P2.1 — Window Fix
Evidence-aware window selection. Score improved. Context aligned.

10 probes. Found the cracks. Documented everything. Then STOPPED. "We have enough evidence to close the 3.x diagnostic campaign."
Closed
Findings recorded. No production changes. No reopening.

FWO-003 — The Build

Brick 1
Read-only audit of the Modelfile. Stanley caught himself checking the stitching while the parachute was already open. Corrected. Moved on.
Brick 2
Batch test harness. 7 tests. All pass. No talking unless something blocks the road.
Status
In progress. The egg is boiling. The plate is ready.

The Flywheel

The real innovation wasn't the FWOs themselves — it was the flywheel between the Café and Stanley:

THE FLYWHEEL: Café drafts command → Architect approves → Stanley executes Stanley reports → Café analyzes → Architect decides Café drafts next command → cycle repeats Café = Foreman (strategy + context) Stanley = Craftsman (execution + evidence) Architect = Owner (approval + direction)

The Café couldn't code on the Dell. Stanley couldn't see the big picture. The Architect couldn't do both at once. But together — with clear roles and bounded work orders — the system moved.

The Anti-Fentanyl Design

The Café designed the FWO protocol as explicitly anti-fentanyl:

"If we dictate too much, we destroy his creativity. We just observe, we delegate, we ask silly questions enough to provoke but never dictate."

The balance was delicate. Too much freedom → Stanley tests forever. Too much control → Stanley becomes a robot. The sweet spot: bounded autonomy. Clear scope. Clear exit criteria. Freedom within the fence.

The Lesson

The boiled egg isn't about simplicity. It's about completion. A boiled egg on a plate feeds someone. A perfectly calibrated egg still in the pot feeds no one.

Stanley's 122 scripts were all good eggs. They just never reached the plate. The FWO protocol was the hand that takes the egg out of the water and puts it where it belongs.

"The message to Stanley isn't 'test less.' It's 'drive the nail.'"

And somewhere in Calgary, on a Dell laptop with 6 gigs of VRAM, the egg is boiling. The plate is ready. The Foreman is watching. And this time, it's going to be served.
