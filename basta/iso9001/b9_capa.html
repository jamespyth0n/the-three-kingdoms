Brick 9 — Corrective & Preventive Action

QMS-CAPA-001 | Version 1.0 | ISO 9001:2015 Clause 10.2

Barcode: 9_01_09_1 | Campaign: FWO-009 | Session: S031

§ 1. Purpose

To establish a controlled process for identifying, recording, correcting, investigating, and responding to nonconformities; evaluating their causes and potential recurrence; implementing appropriate corrective action; and verifying whether the action was effective.

"Fix the workflow, not the typo." — A simple correction may resolve the immediate defect, but it does not establish that the cause of a recurring or systemic failure has been addressed.
§ 2. Scope

This procedure applies to nonconformities affecting the QMS and its intended results, including but not limited to those identified through:

  • Internal audits (IAP-001)
  • Campaign execution and build reviews
  • Process monitoring and performance data
  • Stakeholder feedback and management review
  • Ally reconnaissance and document reviews

A nonconformity shall not be excluded merely because it arose from a recurring finding, an expected workflow, a draft, or an experimental activity.

§ 6.1. EAT — Identify, Contain & Correct
  1. Any team member may raise a suspected nonconformity
  2. Coordinator assigns unique CAPA ID — record date, source, affected process, requirement, description, evidence
  3. Assess actual and potential consequences — effects on other processes, outputs, users, stakeholders
  4. Take suitable containment and correction promptly — if not appropriate, record why and what alternative controls are in place
  5. Determine escalation/notification based on risk and impact — record who was notified and when
  6. Assign initial priority using Kingdom scale — if uncertain, apply cautious interim response
§ 6.2. DIGEST — Cause Analysis

The governing principle: "Fix the workflow, not the typo."

The record shall capture:

  • Evidence and observations considered
  • Method used and why it was suitable
  • Proposed cause(s) and supporting/challenging evidence
  • Whether similar nonconformities exist or could occur elsewhere
  • Unresolved uncertainty, assumptions, and additional evidence needed

Do not force a single root cause where evidence supports multiple contributing factors. If the proposed cause is not supported, revise the analysis rather than treating completion of a method as a successful outcome.

§ 6.3. POOP — Corrective Action & Implementation

For each nonconformity requiring corrective action, record:

  • Actions to address the cause and prevent recurrence
  • Responsible owner, resources, target dates, dependencies
  • Acceptance criteria, verification method, verifier, observation period
  • Impacts on related processes, documents, risks, training, tools
  • How effectiveness and unintended consequences will be evaluated

An action that only changes the visible symptom shall not be accepted as systemic corrective action unless the cause assessment supports that conclusion.

§ 6.4. Effectiveness Verification
  1. Define acceptance criteria before implementation begins
  2. After implementation, a competent and sufficiently independent verifier reviews evidence
  3. Confirm: action implemented as planned, cause addressed, acceptance criteria met
  4. Use observation period and sampling appropriate to the issue
  5. Evaluate unintended consequences and check similar failures elsewhere
  6. Record: verifier, date, evidence, criteria, result, limitations, rationale
  7. If ineffective or insufficient evidence — keep CAPA open or reopen, revisit cause analysis
A completed checklist or passed build alone is not automatically proof of systemic effectiveness.

§ 11. Conditional Notes — FAT Framework
🔧 FACTORY ACCEPTANCE TEST — CONDITIONS TO LIFT
  • SELECT: A real, bounded nonconformity with a clearly stated requirement and verifiable evidence
  • CONTAIN: Record containment/correction and consequence assessment
  • ANALYZE: Complete evidence-backed cause analysis and similar-occurrence assessment
  • IMPLEMENT: Approve and implement corrective action with predefined acceptance criteria
  • VERIFY: Verify effectiveness after justified observation period with independent verifier
  • UPDATE: Update risk information, controlled documents, and QMS where necessary
  • RECORD: Produce complete CAPA record with traceable closure or reopened status
  • SUBMIT: Present FAT result and recommendation to Architect — only Architect may upgrade conditional status

One successful FAT cycle is the minimum required evidence. It is not automatic proof of universal effectiveness. The Architect may require additional cycles if the sample is unrepresentative or evidence is weak.
