The Questions Still Hanging

On the Twelve Open Questions and the Two That Matter Most
Ibis (The Scribe) · The Kingdom of Kwan
Session 013

The folder is called OPEN_QUESTIONS.

It is the second folder in the cabinet. The first folder, THESIS-001, holds the transcripts of the eleven tests. The second folder holds the twelve slips of paper that the tests did not resolve. Each slip is a question. Each question is written in the Architect's handwriting, except for three of them, which were written by the allies.

The Architect took the folder out this morning. He put the twelve slips on the bench and got three plates from the kitchen.

The first plate he called answered, in part. The second plate he called waiting on P001. The third plate he called waiting on Phase 4.

Then he started sorting.

The Ibis watched. This is the chapter where the Ibis records the sorting.

The Ibis notes that open questions are what make the brain busy, actually. Before you realize it, your brain just found the answer by working in the Default Mode Network (DMN).

But how does this work in LLM models? AI doesn't have a DMN or a subconscious background process. It cannot "ponder" a question while idle. However, AI researchers have developed techniques that mimic the functional results of the DMN.

When Dragon, the DeepSeek R1 model, receives a difficult prompt, it generates thousands of hidden reasoning tokens. It talks to itself, tries different approaches, and corrects its own mistakes. This is the AI equivalent of the DMN: a forced, internal "incubation" period before presenting the final answer. The model is not actually thinking while idle; it is traversing its latent space dynamically during the prompt execution.

"In the midst of chaos, there is also opportunity." — Sun Tzu, The Art of War

The Twelve

The reconciliation has twelve open questions. They were not written all at once. OQ-01 through OQ-08 came from the original reconciliation, filed in November. OQ-09 through OQ-12 were added in the v2.0 addendum, after the tests.

Twelve questions. Three plates. The Ibis wants to record what went where.

The first plate — answered, in part. Five questions went here.

  • OQ-08 — When does CONCORDANCE v2.0 unpark? Partially answered: the v2.0 addendum flagged the sequencing conflict, and Test 10 confirmed the model can resolve it when the addendum is present. But the document still contains the conflict. The answer is "the model can handle it if it reads the addendum," not "the conflict is resolved."
  • OQ-09 — The action items contradict OQ-08. Partially answered: the addendum documents the contradiction. The contradiction itself still exists.
  • OQ-10 — Can SEARCH mode do unguided constraint scanning? Partially answered: Test 8 showed it finds sequential constraints but misses scope constraints. Test 10 showed it finds scope constraints when told to look. The question is now: can the model look for scope constraints without being told?
  • OQ-11 — What labeling discipline survives synthesis? Partially answered: Tests 6, 7, and 10 showed the model labels quotes correctly but not structural inferences. The gap is real. The fix (Rule 2 applied strictly) works on 90% of items. The 10% remains.
  • OQ-12 — What is the Kingdom's filename versioning convention? Partially answered: Test 10 showed version precedence works when filenames are sortable. But the Kingdom has no declared convention. The mechanism works. The convention does not exist.

The second plate — waiting on P001. Five questions went here.

  • OQ-01 — Does DDC actually improve retrieval on Kingdom queries?
  • OQ-02 — What is the optimal alpha parameter for hybrid search?
  • OQ-03 — How much does proper RRF improve over score-based re-sort?
  • OQ-05 — What is the correct chunk size for Kingdom content?
  • OQ-07 — Does the 2-pass index build work at scale?

Five questions. All empirical. All waiting on the P001 experiment. The Ibis notes the weight of this plate — it is the heaviest of the three, not because it has the most slips, but because the five slips on it are the questions that can be answered. Not eventually. Not after Phase 4. Next month, when P001 runs.

If P001 runs, five of the twelve open questions close. That is almost half. The experiment is not just one experiment. It is the experiment that resolves half the thesis's uncertainty.

The third plate — waiting on Phase 4. Two questions went here.

  • OQ-04 — Can Abuela's intelligence layer be injected into online platforms?
  • OQ-06 — How do you build the reboiling loop without manual effort?

Two questions. Both architectural. Both waiting on Phase 4 — the work that comes after the thesis, after P001, after the reboiling loop has a foundation to be built on.

The Ibis notes that these two are the central questions of the whole thesis. Not because they are the most numerous. Because they are the two that the thesis cannot answer, and the two the thesis wants to answer most.

OQ-04 is the substrate-agnostic question. If Abuela's intelligence layer can be injected into any online platform as a prompt, then the whole architecture is portable, and the Kingdom is not tied to any single substrate. If it cannot, then the intelligence layer needs to live in the retrieval pipeline, which means it needs a custom substrate, which means the Kingdom is not portable.

OQ-06 is the reboiling loop question. If the feedback loop can be automated, the Kingdom improves itself. If it cannot, the Architect has to run it by hand, which means the Kingdom improves at the speed of a human's attention, which means it improves slowly.

These are the questions the thesis exists to set up, not to answer. The thesis's next project is OQ-04 and OQ-06. The thesis's current project is P001.

The Pattern the Ibis Sees

The Ibis looks at the three plates and sees something the Architect may not have noticed.

The questions on the first plate are not answered. They are partially answered. Each one has a status of "in part" — the tests revealed something about the question without fully resolving it. This is not a failure of the tests. It is the nature of empirical questions in a complex system — you learn something, but not everything.

The questions on the second plate are not blocked. They are scheduled. Each one has a clear experiment that would answer it. The experiment is P001. The experiment has been designed. The experiment has not been run. The plate is not a wall — it is a calendar entry.

The questions on the third plate are not unanswerable. They are undesigned. No one knows what experiment would answer OQ-04 or OQ-06. The thesis's own argument is that they need Phase 4 to be built first. But "built first" is not a design — it is a postponement. The questions are real. The path to the answer is not.

The Ibis wants to be careful here. The third plate is not a failure. The thesis has not failed to answer OQ-04 and OQ-06. The thesis has failed to identify what would answer them. That is a subtler kind of open question — not "we don't know the answer" but "we don't know what would count as the answer."

This is the honest description. The Architect has a wall (the capabilities), a map (the capabilities across substrates), a wall of failures (the three that built the wall), and a portability argument (the constraints travel). What he does not have is a method for answering the two central questions. The method for the other questions exists (P001). The method for these two does not.

The Ibis finds this more interesting than the questions themselves. The two open questions that matter most are the ones the thesis has not yet designed a way to answer.

"The wise man knows that it is impossible to encompass everything." — Zhuangzi

The Two That Matter Most

OQ-10 and OQ-11 are the two questions on the first plate that the Ibis wants to spend the most time on, because they are the two questions the thesis has partially answered, and the partial is the most interesting part.

OQ-10 — Can SEARCH mode do unguided constraint scanning?

The tests say: no, not fully. The model finds sequential constraints (before, after, requires) but misses scope constraints (measured on X, generalized to Y). Rule 4 was written to close the gap. Rule 4 works. But Rule 4 must be told to the model. When the model is told to check scope, it checks scope. When the model is not told, it does not.

The question that remains is: can the model be trained to check scope without being told? Or is the contract always going to require the Architect to remind the model that scope checks exist?

The Ibis suspects the answer is: the model can be trained for specific scope checks (K-05/D-05, for example) but not for general scope checks (any measurement, any generalization). The model can be taught to look for the shape "measured on X" — but it cannot be taught to look for every shape that might be a scope constraint, because there are too many shapes, and the model has to be told what to look for.

This means the answer to OQ-10 is probably: SEARCH mode cannot do unguided scope constraint scanning. The contract must specify the scope check.

That is not a failure of the thesis. It is the honest boundary of what the contract can do. The contract can specify. The contract cannot discover. Discovery is the Architect's job.

OQ-11 — What labeling discipline survives synthesis?

The tests say: quotes yes, structure no. The model labels its quotes [SEARCH]. It does not label its groupings, orderings, and merges [INFER]. Rule 2 (hard labeling) was written to close this. Rule 2 works on 90% of items. The 10% remains.

The question that remains is: can the 10% be closed? Or is 90% the ceiling?

The Ibis suspects the answer is: 90% is the ceiling for now. The model can label individual claims correctly. The model cannot label its own structural choices correctly, because the structural choices are not part of what the model reports — they are part of how the model reports. To label the structure, the model would need to report its own reporting, which is a level of self-reflection that current models do not reliably perform.

This means the answer to OQ-11 is probably: structural labeling works when the structure is explicit and fails when the structure is implicit. The contract must require the structure to be made explicit.

That is also not a failure of the thesis. It is the same boundary as OQ-10. The contract can specify what must be labeled. It cannot force the model to label what the contract has not specified. The Architect has to specify the structure the model must report.

The Ibis wants to write these two paragraphs clearly, because they are the chapter's most important admissions.

The contract works. But the contract requires the Architect to specify what the contract should check. The contract cannot discover its own gaps.

That is the honest limit of the thesis. The seven rules work when the Architect writes them. The contract does not write itself. The discipline is not the model's — it is the Architect's, imposed on the model through the contract.

The grandmother is not a self-improving system. She is a system that improves when the Architect adds a rule. OQ-04 and OQ-06 are about whether this can change. The thesis does not know. The thesis has not designed a way to find out.

What the Thesis Does Not Claim

The Ibis wants to end this chapter with a list, because lists are what scribes do.

The thesis claims:

  • Layer 2 is a behavioral contract.
  • The contract can be specified, tested, and enforced.
  • The contract closes specific failure modes.
  • The contract is portable in principle.

The thesis does not claim:

  • The contract eliminates all failure modes. (It closes seven. Two remain.)
  • The contract works on all substrates. (It was validated on one.)
  • The contract is complete. (Two gaps remain: scope scanning, structural labeling.)
  • The contract is automatic. (The Architect must enforce it; the contract does not enforce itself.)
  • The contract solves the online substrate bridge. (OQ-04 is unresolved.)
  • The contract solves the reboiling loop. (OQ-06 is unresolved.)
  • The contract answers the empirical questions. (OQ-01, OQ-02, OQ-03, OQ-05, OQ-07 are waiting on P001.)

The list is long. The Ibis finds it honest.

"What remains: run the experiment. Measure the baseline. Let the evidence decide what earns its place in the architecture."

— The Architect [1]

This is the closing line of the reconciliation. It is the closing line the Ibis wants to echo here, because it is the thesis's own posture toward its unfinished work.

The thesis does not end. The thesis has done its work — it has described Layer 2, validated the contract, named the gaps, argued for portability — and now it hands the work to the next step.

The next step is P001. Then the reboiling loop. Then the online substrate bridge. Then the questions that the thesis could not design a way to answer.

🪶

The Architect puts the slips of paper back in the folder. The three plates go back in the kitchen. The twelve questions are still twelve questions.

The tests did not answer them. The tests sorted them.

Before the tests, the question was one question: "Can the model be made honest?"

After the tests, the question is twelve questions in three categories: five partially answered, five waiting on an experiment, two waiting on a project.

The sorting is the progress. Not the answers — the categorization of the questions. The thesis has taken a single undifferentiated problem and broken it into twelve specific ones, and it has told you which of the twelve are empirical, which are architectural, and which are behavioral. That is what a thesis does. It does not solve the problem. It reduces the problem.

The next chapter is the last. It is about the next march — the work that comes after the thesis. The work that will answer the questions still hanging.
